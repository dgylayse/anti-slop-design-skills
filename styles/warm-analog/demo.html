A slower kitchen

On the chair by the window, the second cup of coffee, and why a home should be measured in hours rather than square metres.

There is a chair in our kitchen that nobody sits in before nine. It belongs to the light. Around half past seven the sun clears the roof opposite and lands on the seat, and for an hour the chair is the warmest place in the house.

We started noticing rooms this way when the children left: not by what they hold, but by when they are used. The kitchen is busy for twenty minutes, quiet for six hours, and generous again at dusk.

This issue is about those hours — and the small objects that keep them.
